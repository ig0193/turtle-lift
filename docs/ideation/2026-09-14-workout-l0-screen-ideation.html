Grounding Context

Codebase Context

The Workout tab root is WorkoutRoot(), mounted in L0Shell, which owns the app's only Scaffold. It currently renders a placeholder EmptyState with a barbell glyph and the copy “Ready when you are”; its hasSession flag is not yet wired to a provider. The landing UI is build-order step 4 and has not been built.

Four constraints shape every candidate below:

  • The header is pinned and outside the body. WorkoutRoot returns a scrollable body, not a Scaffold; no title row may live inside the scroll view. The tab bar floats over content and appears only at L0.
  • There is no Resume card. Under navigation variant A, an active session replaces the tab root entirely, so this screen is purely the no-session-open state.
  • Nothing derivable is stored. Streak, set and exercise counts, personal bests, the trained tick and the resolved session title are computed on every read, because sessions are editable, deletable and backdatable.
  • Eleven colours exist, and two carry fixed meanings. #D85A30 means “active / trained / this has been worked” wherever it appears; #F0997B is secondary-muscle only; #C4553A is destructive only. A test fails on any colour outside the set.

Nine predefined templates and 260 exercises ship inside the binary, so day one is not empty of content — only of history. The body diagram's 42 segments are already built as hit-testable point lists and are used nowhere yet.

External Context

Three schools of workout-app home screen recur in the market. Tool-first (Strong) makes home a single start action plus a short routines list, preferred by people who log fast and leave. Feed-first (Hevy) leads with a social activity feed — ruled out here, as there is no cloud or login. Plan-first (Fitbod, Boostcamp, Apple Fitness+) surfaces a prescribed workout derived from history — ruled out by the no-AI and no-multi-day-splits constraints. None of the three leads with a dashboard of metrics; streaks and PBs are shown small or pushed to a secondary tab.

Habit research makes the streak the primary retention lever, but via notifications and badges — both excluded. A computed streak can be shown; it can never be pushed. Separately, no competitor reviewed puts a body diagram on its landing screen, which is either an unfilled gap or a signal that it does not belong there.

Topic Axes

  1. The start decision — how the two mandated paths are presented and weighted, and how deep template content surfaces on L0 itself.
  2. The look back — what the screen says about past training: streak, last session, recency.
  3. The look forward — what the screen says about what to train next: muscle coverage, the body diagram.
  4. Day one vs month six — how the screen behaves across data volume, from zero sessions to heavy use.

Open Rulings

Four things surfaced during grounding that are decisions, not design preferences. They are reported rather than resolved, because each needs a call that is not this document's to make.

1. The two prototypes disagree on entry-point weighting

prototypes/screens.html:322-323 renders “Start workout” as .big (filled, primary) with “Ad-hoc workout” as .ghost. The untracked prototypes/v1-end-to-end.html:844,854 gives both an identical .card-btn. Both citations were verified.

CLAUDE.md's rule is “where they differ on how a screen looks, follow the prototype” — which has no tiebreaker when two prototypes differ from each other. Every variant below has to pick a side, so this is worth settling first.

2. L0 is not on the diagram's list of permitted surfaces

docs/00-build-spec.md:112 enumerates where the body diagram may appear — Overview, Muscles landing, sub-muscle-group selection, Exercise Detail, summary and session cards. The Workout tab root is not among them.

Variants 4 and 6 need that list amended before they can ship. This is a documented rule, not an oversight, so it should be changed deliberately or the variants dropped.

3. Quick log has no fast route

A single-exercise quick log is a first-class session type: it counts toward the streak (docs/00-build-spec.md:144), gets its own rung in the title-resolution chain (docs/01-app-idea.md:208), and is labelled distinctly in History (docs/04-profile-tab-userflow.md:38). It is reachable today only by going through the ad-hoc path, which creates an implicit session (docs/01-app-idea.md:198).

So the gap is narrower than “no entry point” — but there is no dedicated fast route to the thing three documents describe as its own session shape.

4. The grace-day hint belongs to Profile, not here

docs/00-build-spec.md:148 permits an “optional in-app hint on the final grace day. No notifications.” That line names no surface. docs/01-app-idea.md:251 does: “Profile can show a ‘streak ends tomorrow’ hint.”

Per CLAUDE.md, 01 wins over 00. An early version of variant 5 claimed this screen as the hint's natural home; that claim does not survive. The streak may still be shown at L0 — it just cannot borrow this line as its warrant.

Ranked Ideas

The seven entries below are composed screens, not loose suggestions — each assembles several surviving candidate ideas into something that could be built as-is. They are ranked, but the top three are genuinely different philosophies rather than better and worse versions of one thing, so read at least those three before choosing.

Directional only

The mockups illustrate the intended shape of each screen. Exact spacing, copy and component choices are placeholders for review, not requirements. They do use the real §12 palette rather than grey boxes, because several variants turn on palette semantics — whether a streak may be orange at all, given that #D85A30 already means “this has been worked” — and a greyscale wireframe cannot show that. The body silhouette in variant 6 is a rough stand-in for the traced asset, not the asset itself.

  2. The Tool — two doors, nothing else

V1

The Service Tag

Top pick

Two weighted entry points and exactly one line of memory: what you did last and how long ago. Nothing else derived, nothing that congratulates.

Axis
The look back
Confidence
86%
Complexity
Low
Assembles
Recall-not-recommendation · settled door weighting · three shipping states

DescriptionThe screen carries the two mandated paths with a clear primary/secondary hierarchy, plus a single line resolving the last session's title and its recency. The line uses the existing four-rung title fallback chain, so it is never blank for any session that exists: a named session shows its name, an untitled ad-hoc session falls through to its comma-separated primary muscle groups, a quick log shows the exercise name. Three states key off session count and days elapsed — fresh (explanatory copy, no recall line), warm (recall line, copy dropped), lapsed (neutral recall, streak suppressed entirely, no guilt copy).

Basisdirect: docs/02-workout-tab-userflow.md:7 — “the app does not suggest or auto-select a workout. There is no ‘today's workout,’ no rotation logic, no AI… The user always makes an active choice.” Recall is legal where recommendation is not. direct: docs/00-build-spec.md:126-131, the title fallback chain ending “Primary muscle groups trained, comma-separated”, verified. direct: lib/src/ui/empty_state.dart:7-13 — “This is a shipping screen state, not a placeholder… every user meets this on day one and keeps meeting it until they log something.”

RationaleThis is the smallest change that makes the screen say something specific to this user without the app ever advising. The plan-first competitors answer “what do I train today” with logic this project has banned outright; recall is the only legal answer, and it costs one query against a value the History tab already needs. It also lets the user perform the inference the app is forbidden to perform.

DownsidesIt settles for less than the screen could do — a user wanting coverage or progress sees neither. The lapsed state needs history and absence together, so it is the state most likely to ship untested. And it still inherits Ruling 1: the door weighting has to be decided.

Why this ranks first

Every citation verified, no constraint conflicts, no open ruling blocking it beyond the weighting question every variant shares, and the lowest build cost of the seven. It is also the only variant that is straightforwardly compatible with adopting a second one later — the recall line coexists with a stamp card, a chip strip, or a diagram, where several other variants compete for the same real estate.

V2

The Tool

Restraint pole

No backward-looking content at all, written down as a decision rather than left as an omission. Two doors, a stock line, and nothing to read.

Axis
The start decision
Confidence
80%
Complexity
Low
Assembles
Zero look-back · settled door weighting · stock behind glass

DescriptionRule out every backward-looking element on this screen permanently — no streak, no last-session line, no counts of what you have done — and record that as a decision. The one derived string is a count of what sits behind each door: nine templates rising as the user saves customs, 260 exercises. Day one therefore makes a claim about the tool rather than about the user's empty history.

Basisexternal: Strong's home screen is a single start action plus a short routines list; convergent review sentiment is that it suits people who log fast and leave, and that none of the three market schools leads with metrics. direct: lib/src/ui/workout_root.dart:44-50 — the current heading “Ready when you are” says nothing about anything being stocked, though nine templates and 260 exercises ship in the binary. reasoned: once one backward-looking line exists, “just one more” is an easy next ask; naming the boundary once is cheaper than re-litigating it per element.

RationaleThis screen is opened standing in a gym, often between sets, and its job is to be left quickly. Everything that is not an action is a tax on that. The stock counts also solve the empty-state problem without any history: an app that says “260 exercises” on first open does not read as empty, and because both counts only grow, the number can never become discouraging.

DownsidesA user with three months of history gets no payoff for it at the screen they open most. It is also the variant most likely to feel inert — the deliberate cost of the position. If the team later wants any derived content here, this decision has to be reopened rather than extended.

The case against ranking this first

It is the safest variant and the cheapest, and the market evidence genuinely supports it. It ranks second only because V1 buys a meaningful amount of life for one extra query and one line of text, and the “no derived content, ever” position is easier to adopt later than to reverse.

V3

The Learned Default

Biggest tap win

The primary button is named after the template you last ran, and goes straight to its overview — skipping the picker for the common case without ever prescribing a workout.

Axis
Day one vs month six
Confidence
74%
Complexity
Medium
Assembles
Learned default · settled door weighting · stock behind glass

DescriptionFor a user with at least one completed session, compute the most recently used templateId — one query, nothing stored — and label the primary action with that template's name, routing straight to its overview. A secondary text link preserves the picker for the exception case, and the ad-hoc door is unchanged. Users with no history see today's generic flow, so day one is untouched.

Basisdirect: docs/00-build-spec.md:169 names the picker as the template entry point, making the picker step the thing being skipped. reasoned: a repeat user is disproportionately likely to re-run the template they last ran, so collapsing that step is a pure tap saving against the stated three-tap budget, costing nothing for a first-time user. It remains a plain “last used” default — not a schedule, not a rotation, not a prescription — which keeps it clear of the ruled-out plan-first school.

RationaleIt is the only variant that makes the screen behave differently at month six than on day one on purpose, and it targets the median path rather than an edge. The distinction that makes it legal is narrow but real: the app is forbidden from choosing a workout for you, not from remembering the one you chose.

DownsidesThe edge cases need rulings before it can be built — what happens when the last-used template has been deleted, renamed, or was a one-off custom. There is also a genuine question of whether naming a template on the button reads as the app suggesting it, which is exactly the line the project has drawn carefully elsewhere.

Compatibility

This composes cleanly with V1's recall line (the two say different things — one names what you did, the other what you would repeat) but overlaps awkwardly with it, since “Start Push day” and “Last session · Push day” would often show the same words. Pick one or resolve the redundancy.

V4

The Open Stack

Boldest

Stop asking which path before the user has any information. Put the search field and the actual template rows on the landing, and let the fork happen by what they tap.

Axis
The start decision
Confidence
62%
Complexity
High
Assembles
Open stacks · stock made visible rather than counted

DescriptionReplace the two card-buttons with one composition surface: a live exercise-search field above the actual template rows, in the picker's own row shape. Tapping a template goes to its overview; typing goes to ad-hoc composition. The template-versus-ad-hoc distinction stops being a question asked up front and becomes a consequence of what the user touches. Because the nine shipped templates are visible, the stock count of variant 2 becomes redundant — the inventory is the evidence.

Basisdirect: docs/02-workout-tab-userflow.md:21 — both paths “converge into the same underlying session-running experience from Step 2 onward, just with different starting inputs”, so the Step 1 divergence is a UI choice rather than a structural one. direct: docs/01-app-idea.md:349 — the minimal-typing rule is scoped to “steppers/number inputs”, i.e. numeric entry, and does not argue against search. external: the library shift from closed to open stacks traded curatorial control for fewer intermediating steps; the analogy holds because the catalogue here is small enough to fit on the shelf, which is precisely why the closed-stack rationale is weaker here than in the domain it comes from.

RationaleIt is the largest available tap-budget win and it makes day one rich rather than apologetic. It also removes the one moment on this screen where a beginner can pick wrong, since they are choosing between things they can see rather than between two abstractions.

DownsidesThe heaviest of the seven, and it partly absorbs a screen the docs specify separately (docs/02-workout-tab-userflow.md:125 names a Workout template picker). The verifier also flagged that the claimed tap saving only holds if switching tabs counts as a tap — plausible, but it was never stated. And the search field competes with the template rows for first-glance attention, which is the real risk: two affordances at the top of a screen whose job is to be left quickly.

Conflict to resolve first

This variant and the separate template-picker screen cannot both be the answer. Adopting it means either deleting that screen from the flow or redefining it as the “all 9 templates” overflow destination, which is the lighter of the two readings and probably the right one.

V5

The Stamp Card

Needs Ruling 1

Fourteen days as cells, with the chain drawn underneath — so the screen shows not just what the streak is, but why it is that number.

Axis
The look back
Confidence
58%
Complexity
Medium
Assembles
Stamp card with the chain drawn · settled door weighting · three shipping states

DescriptionA row of cells, one per recent calendar day, filled when that day has at least one completed set. The part that distinguishes it from a generic contribution heatmap is the hairline beneath: it joins consecutive filled days whose gap falls within the three-day tolerance, and visibly breaks where it does not. The number and the picture explain each other. On day one it is a row of empty cells — a ruled card, not an error grid.

Basisdirect: docs/01-app-idea.md:247-249 — the streak is a pure function of the set of distinct performedOn dates with a three-day tolerance, so both the fills and the chain derive from the walk the streak computation already performs. external: the date-due card in the back of a library book — blank it reads as a promise rather than a defect, full it reads as a record, and the same artifact serves day one and year ten without changing shape.

RationaleThe three-day tolerance is the app's most misunderstandable mechanic, and this explains it with no copy at all. Heatmaps elsewhere show density; this shows the rule. It is also the only look-back element here that is honestly good at both ends of the data range, since an empty card still communicates the shape of what will fill it.

DownsidesIt runs directly at a written rule: docs/03-muscle-groups-tab-userflow.md:57-61 warns “Do not render three tiles of dashes. An empty grid reads as broken; one line reads as a state.” The card survives that objection only if empty cells genuinely read as ruling rather than as missing values, which is a judgement that needs to be made against a running screen, not a mockup. It is also the most visually assertive of the look-back options on a screen meant to be left quickly.

What changed after verification

An earlier version of this variant paired the card with a grace-window countdown, justified by docs/00-build-spec.md:148 (“Optional in-app hint on the final grace day. No notifications.”). That line is real, but docs/01-app-idea.md:251 assigns the hint to Profile, and 01 outranks 00. The countdown was removed; see Ruling 4.

V6

The Record

Needs Ruling 2

The body diagram on the landing, lit from the last completed session and captioned with its date. It states position, not course.

Axis
The look forward
Confidence
55%
Complexity
Medium
Assembles
Diagram as record · recall line · settled door weighting

DescriptionRender the standard full-body diagram, unmodified viewBox, filled from the last completed session's primary and secondary muscle map, with its date beneath. The framing matters: this is a record of what happened, not a readiness map. It implies no recovery state, because none is modelled. With zero sessions it renders nothing rather than an empty body.

Basisdirect: docs/00-build-spec.md:119 — the share-card view rule (“show only the view(s) containing trained muscles… Never render an empty second diagram”) already solves the empty case, verified. direct: the body_paths.dart header — 42 segments as point lists with a toPath() helper and a caching note; the render path exists and is used nowhere yet. external: no reviewed competitor (Strong, Hevy, Fitbod) puts a muscle map on a landing screen — they keep it in summaries or a separate tab.

RationaleIt is the app's strongest visual asset, already built, currently absent from its most-viewed screen. The read-as-record framing is what neutralises the usual objection, which is that a muscle map on a home screen implies recovery tracking the app deliberately refuses to do. And the competitive gap cuts both ways: either nobody has filled it, or everybody has concluded it does not belong. That is worth deciding with evidence.

Downsidesdocs/00-build-spec.md:112 enumerates the diagram's permitted surfaces and L0 is not among them, so this cannot ship without amending that list (Ruling 2). It also consumes the most vertical space of any element here, pushing the actions out of the thumb arc unless the diagram is kept small. One detail matters: because the palette has no fade, the diagram must be captioned with its date rather than dimmed with age — dimming would imply a decay the data model does not have.

The no-diagram treatment of the same slot

If Ruling 2 goes the other way, the same real estate has a cheaper answer: set the derived last-trained muscle string at display size — CHEST · SHOULDERS · TRICEPS — as the largest object on the screen, with the actions beneath. That exact string is already a specified derived value (it is the final rung of the title fallback chain at docs/00-build-spec.md:126-131), it costs no assets, it reads at two metres on a gym floor, and it parses faster than a picture. Day one holds the existing heading in the same slot. Treat this as the same decision with two renderings, not a separate variant.

V7

The Shadow Board

Needs Ruling 2

Twelve muscle groups as chips, filled if trained in the last seven days. The unfilled ones carry the information; the screen never tells you what to do about them.

Axis
The look forward
Confidence
52%
Complexity
Medium
Assembles
Shadow board · settled door weighting · three shipping states

DescriptionA strip of twelve chips, one per parent muscle group, filled in the accent when any session in the last seven calendar days completed an exercise whose primary muscles include that group, and left in the muted fill otherwise. No ranking, no counts, no ordering by how overdue anything is. The absent fills do the work.

Basisdirect: docs/02-workout-tab-userflow.md:51-57 — trained “is binary and read-only… It gates nothing… Muscle groups are a menu of options, not a checklist to complete”, verified. reasoned: the palette provides exactly two fills that can carry this meaning and nothing between them, so a staleness gradient is not expressible even if it were wanted; the constraint forces binary, which happens to be the only honest encoding of a value that is itself binary. external: the Toyota 5S shadow board, where a tool's painted outline makes its absence self-evident from geometry alone, and the board never instructs.

RationaleThis is the only way found to deliver forward-looking value using nothing but backward-looking facts: no recommendation, no rotation, no AI, no gradient. The user draws the inference the app is forbidden from drawing, and the mechanism for doing so is a query the data model already supports.

DownsidesIt sits close to the “muscle-neglect alert” the project deliberately has nowhere, and the line between a fact and an alert is exactly what needs ruling. Twelve chips is also a lot of visual noise above the actions. And there is a naming trap: the field is called trained rather than done for a reason, so the strip must never acquire a completion count or a progress bar, or it becomes the checklist the docs explicitly refuse.

Citation correction

An earlier draft of this idea presented “no gradient, decay or staleness ramp” as a direct quote from the docs. It is not — that phrasing appears nowhere in docs/. The underlying palette argument holds, but it is a reasoned: inference from the eleven-colour set, not documented text, and has been relabelled accordingly.

Modifiers

These are rules and small additions that apply to any variant rather than competing with them. They survived critique but are not themselves screens.

ModifierWhat it says
ErgonomicsThis is the one screen opened standing, one-handed, often between sets; every other screen is read stationary. Actions belong in the thumb arc — the lower part of the scroll body, above the floating tab bar — not top-aligned in a reading layout. Every mockup above follows this.

Seen three hundred timesNothing on this screen congratulates, animates on entry, or reacts to the user. Explanatory copy appears only in the zero-history state and is dropped once a session exists.
